# PhyVision Dataset Card

**Author:** Swayam Shakya · **Version:** 5.1 · **License:** CC BY 4.0 (images and masks), MIT (generator code)

## Summary

16,000 AI-generated images of fictional people, labelled with one of four physique classes, each paired with a pixel-perfect segmentation mask. Version 5.1 adds varied poses, slight body turns and photo framings from head-to-feet down to waist-up, so the set looks more like the photos people actually upload.

The images are computer-generated by PhyVision's renderer (`src/phyvision/synth.py`); no photographs of real people were used. The generator was built with help from Claude, an AI model by Anthropic, as a learning aid for creating computer-vision training data.

| Split | Athletic | Normal | Obese | Skinny | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| train | 2,000 | 2,000 | 2,000 | 2,000 | 8,000 |
| val | 1,000 | 1,000 | 1,000 | 1,000 | 4,000 |
| test | 1,000 | 1,000 | 1,000 | 1,000 | 4,000 |

## Why synthetic?

There is no openly licensed collection of thousands of labelled photos per body type that can be redistributed. Commercial stock libraries (such as Shutterstock or Dreamstime) license their images and do not allow them to be redistributed in a dataset, and collecting people's photos without consent is not acceptable. Rendering people from parameters gives exact labels, exact masks, balanced classes, full control over diversity, and no privacy risk. The cost is a domain gap to real photographs, which PhyVision reduces by classifying silhouettes rather than raw pixels (see the model card).

## How an image is made

`src/phyvision/synth.py`:

1. **Sex** (55% male / 45% female) selects base half-widths for shoulders, chest, waist, hips, neck, arms and legs, as fractions of body height.
2. **Class modifiers** scale those widths. In v5.1 they were recalibrated against adult anthropometric ratios so the classes look like real people rather than caricatures. For a 1.75 m man, upper-arm circumference is about 24 cm (Skinny), 31 cm (Normal), 40 cm (Athletic) and 42 cm (Obese). Skinny keeps most of the bone frame (shoulders about 7% narrower) with thin limbs and a narrow waist; Athletic widens shoulders by about 14% and limbs by 18 to 30% with a normal waist; Obese widens the waist by about 60%, adds a belly term and thicker limbs. Every modifier is drawn from a normal distribution, so neighbouring classes overlap.
3. **Pose:** each arm is posed separately: down, slightly out, hands on hips, hand in pocket, bicep flex (with a contracted, larger biceps) or raised overhead. Mixes include one-arm flex, double flex, one or both arms raised and mixed poses. Leg spread varies, 40% of people are turned slightly (one half of the body narrowed by up to 18%, like a 3/4 view), and a slight horizontal squash mimics imperfect frontal views.
4. **Appearance:** 12 skin tones, 9 hair colours and 5 hair styles, tops (none, tank, sports top, T-shirt, long sleeve), bottoms (shorts, capri, trousers), random colours, and loose clothing that widens the outline.
5. **Framing:** 50% head to feet, 15% head to knees (72-82% of the body visible), 20% head to mid-thigh (57-70%) and 15% waist up (47-56%). Portrait aspect ratio 0.62-0.82 for full-body photos and 0.68-0.95 for cropped ones.
6. **Scene:** plain white or light-grey stock-photo backdrop (25%), studio gradient, room, gym or outdoor background with clutter, contact shadow (full-body shots), lighting gradient, camera roll, blur, brightness change, sensor noise and, on 15% of images, a faint stock-photo style watermark.

`metadata.csv` records sex, framing, visible body fraction, arm poses (overall and per arm), body turn, clothing, background, image width and height, and the generator's true shoulder-to-waist and waist-to-hip ratios.

## Splits

Seeds are disjoint per split and class (`split offset + class offset + index`), so no person appears in two splits. Regenerate exactly with `python scripts/generate_dataset.py`.

## Known limitations

* Stylised figures, not photographs. Faces, hands and clothing folds are simplified.
* Front-facing (up to a slight turn), standing poses only. No side, back, sitting or lying poses.
* Classes are defined by body proportions, which is a simplification of real body composition.
* In cropped framings, wide poses (an arm held out or flexed) sometimes extend past the edge of the image. When such a body is also turned slightly, the turn effect can leave a straight cut a little inside the frame instead of at the edge. Images and masks are affected the same way, so labels stay correct, but those images are less photo-like.
